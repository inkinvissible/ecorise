# Ecorise Design System

## Design intent

Ecorise should feel like an engineering and energy advisory firm with a strong, recognizable brand: precise, contemporary, technical and visually confident. Avoid both extremes: the generic renewable-energy-template look and the generic SaaS/editorial consultancy look.

The website should inherit the visual DNA already present in Ecorise's social communication without reproducing Instagram cards literally. The goal is continuity of brand across channels.

## Visual world

- Deep teal and near-black are the main structural colors.
- Bright mint is the high-energy accent used for actions, labels, separators and selected graphic moments.
- White is used to preserve clarity and give the stronger brand colors room to breathe.
- Photography should show real infrastructure, panels, industrial/agricultural environments and existing Ecorise assets.
- Graphic language may use diagonal/angular geometric forms inspired by the Ecorise identity and social content.
- Typography is bold and direct when communicating a key idea, but body copy remains calm and readable.
- Layout should alternate editorial whitespace with stronger branded blocks. It should not look like a dashboard or a sequence of floating SaaS cards.

## Social-brand DNA

Recurring visual characteristics visible in Ecorise communication:

- dark teal fields;
- blue-teal secondary fields;
- vivid mint highlights;
- white high-contrast typography;
- strong blocks of text over technical photography;
- numbers and short statements given visual prominence;
- diagonal or arrow-like geometric elements;
- mostly square or lightly rounded surfaces rather than soft pill-heavy UI.

On the website these cues should be translated into heroes, section transitions, CTAs, labels and image treatments, not copied as social-media tiles.

## Typography

- Display and headings: Montserrat, bold, controlled negative tracking.
- Body: Cabin, 65–75ch where possible.
- Key statements may use tighter line-height and larger scale, reflecting Ecorise's social communication.
- Avoid turning every label into all caps. Uppercase or tracked labels are reserved for short brand signals and metadata.

## Components

- Navigation: white and compact, with a mint active indicator.
- Hero: technical photography, deep teal/near-black overlay, bold proposition, one primary CTA and at most one secondary action. Angular brand geometry may appear as a restrained decorative layer.
- Brand ribbon: short high-level concepts such as Analysis, Strategy, Engineering and Energy can act as a visual transition rather than another content section.
- Cards: lightly squared, thin borders and small mint graphic accents. Avoid overly rounded floating cards.
- Sector cards: image-led, dark overlay, bright mint category label and a graphic top rule.
- Processes: technical/timeline feel with angular markers rather than generic circular step dots.
- CTA surfaces: deep teal with mint accent and restrained geometry.
- Buttons: compact radius, vivid mint primary action, strong contrast and clear focus state.
- Browser details: branded focus ring, selection color and link underline behavior.

## Motion

Use motion sparingly. Prefer one intentional image or underline movement rather than repeated entrance animations on every section. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## Responsive behavior

Desktop layouts may use asymmetric columns. On mobile, content becomes a single readable flow, primary CTA expands when helpful, the brand ribbon becomes a 2 × 2 grid and no horizontal overflow is acceptable.

## Content rules

- Never fabricate savings, ROI, payback, clients or project outcomes.
- Avoid positioning Ecorise as merely a solar-panel installer.
- Explain technical decisions in business language without losing engineering credibility.
- Sustainability language can appear when relevant, but the current core narrative is energy strategy, solar, engineering, operation and performance.
- Visual emphasis should reinforce the idea that Ecorise analyzes first and recommends technology second.
